Case study 01

Redesigning a complex enterprise workflow.

A role-based application for global vehicle feature management, designed for the UK market while considering other market groups.

The challenge

The legacy application contained complex functionality and workflows. Users needed clearer steps and more explicit actions, while the experience had to support different responsibilities and consider broader market needs.

My role

End-to-end UX work across requirements gathering, user research, information architecture, persona creation, user journeys, prototyping, and usability testing. During an onsite UK assignment, I engaged directly with users to understand pain points and expectations.

Scope at a glance

EPIC 01
Admin

Role-specific tasks and controls

EPIC 02
Carline Analyst

Analyst-focused workflows

EPIC 03
PI users

Dedicated role-based journeys

Approximately 20 flows per epic, around 60 overall. At roughly 5–10 screens per flow, the estimated scope is 300–600 screen instances; counts are indicative and may include repeated patterns.

01 · DiscoverGather requirements and understand user pain points and expectations.
02 · StructureOrganize information, define personas, and map user journeys.
03 · DesignSimplify workflows and make actions more explicit.
04 · ValidateUse usability testing and feedback to refine the experience.

Design decisions

  • Break complex functionality into clearer workflows.
  • Make action-specific decisions and next steps easier to understand.
  • Design for the UK market while considering other market groups.
  • Apply accessibility principles and UX standards.

What changed

The redesigned experience made functionality more straightforward and easier for users to learn and adapt to. The simplified process was intended to support work efficiency and accuracy.

Design should reduce the effort needed to understand what to do next.

Stakeholders and senior management have appreciated the work. No quantified performance results are claimed.
